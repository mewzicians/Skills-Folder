# Audition Method

Read this before building or interpreting any listening comparison, in any
medium: a rendered file, a score playback, a session bounce, or a code-driven
page. For browser audio, also read
[web-audio-auditions.md](web-audio-auditions.md).

## Choose The Audition

Use a discovery audition to compare genuinely different identities or
directions. Use a diagnostic audition to test one specific cause.

Build a comparison only around a clearly audible discriminator or a named
defect it would resolve. Small differences in gain, filtering, subtle layer
density, or overall energy are often not perceptible, so a comparison built on
them can cost a full listening round and return no verdict. Candidates must
differ audibly in the named variable; cosmetic variants presented as distinct
ideas waste the listener's attention.

## Run A Diagnostic Comparison

1. Name the question.
2. Preserve an approved or exact control.
3. Hold irrelevant variables constant.
4. Change one meaningful variable or one coordinated causal hypothesis.
5. Use neutral candidate names, and do not tell the listener what to feel
   before the first listen.
6. Match perceived loudness unless loudness is the variable being tested.
7. Listen through complete phrases or forms, with enough repeat exposure to
   reveal fatigue, appetite, or loop behavior.
8. Capture the unprompted first reaction before explaining the hypothesis.
9. State what each possible result would change in the next decision.
10. Keep controls for pause, replay, seeking, timestamps, and direct switching
    when the medium supports them.

## The Audition Contract

Every substantive recommendation that selects a direction, diagnoses a cause,
or claims readiness carries a compact contract:

- the exact control;
- the changed variable or coordinated causal hypothesis;
- the audible decision criterion;
- what each plausible result changes next;
- the repeated complete-phrase or complete-form exposure; and
- the current status plus remaining subjective, technical, delivery, or
  integration gates.

Raw brainstorming that makes no recommendation or readiness claim may omit it.
Brevity is not a reason to omit it from a substantive decision.

## Interpret The Result

Interpret the user's unprompted reaction before explaining what the music was
meant to communicate. Treat a disliked sound, melody, arrangement, or
production choice as taste evidence, not a defect; call something a technical
defect only after reproduction or source-level evidence. When a verdict
accepts part of a candidate and rejects part, record each part separately
instead of promoting or rejecting the whole (see
[artifact-lifecycle.md](artifact-lifecycle.md)).

## Prepare The Next Round

Build the next audition from the selected material. Leave every compared
version byte-intact as evidence of what was heard. Retiring, moving, or
replacing a compared candidate is a lifecycle change that needs the user's
explicit approval naming both the exact item and the action.
